# 00_VISION_AND_RULES


## 1. Project Vision
A single-player, browser-based 3D space exploration sandbox. The player flies a spaceship with Newtonian physics through a compressed but real-angle solar system, lands on procedural terrain-covered planets and moons, and can exit the ship to walk or drift as an astronaut.
*   **Visual Target:** Calm, soft, atmospheric (atmospheric scattering, gentle color grading, altitude fog).
*   **Performance Target:** 60 FPS on mid-range laptops at Medium settings. Absolute Zero-Garbage-Collection (Zero-GC) stutter during continuous 2,000 m/s flight.
*   **Platform:** Desktop web browser. Keyboard and mouse only.


## 2. Core Technical Philosophy
1.  **The Engine Split:** Three.js is *only* the renderer. The simulation core is pure TypeScript running in 64-bit doubles (`Float64`). No Three.js objects (`Vector3`, `Matrix4`) may exist inside the simulation core.
2.  **Two-Tier Time:** The solar system is massive, and running physics at 30x time scale melts CPUs. 
    *   *Macro-Scale:* Planets, moons, and portals move on closed-form Kepler orbital rails. They are evaluated analytically as a function of `simTime` (which runs at 30x).
    *   *Micro-Scale:* The local 2 km Rapier physics bubble runs in real-time (1x) at exactly 60 Hz. 
3.  **The Math Overrides the Engine:** If a ship flies at 2,000 m/s, it outruns async collider generation. Therefore, high-speed flight relies on exact 2D DDA mathematical raycasting against the noise function on the Main Thread, *not* Rapier colliders. Nothing is allowed to clip, tunnel, or NaN.


## 3. Strict Project Constraints (The Laws)
*   **Stack:** WebGL2 (Three.js), React Three Fiber (R3F), Rapier (physics), Zustand (UI state). No other libraries without explicit approval.
*   **Scale Rule (WorldScale = 1):** 1 game unit = 1 metre. Earth's game radius is exactly 5,000 m. All sizes, distances, and speeds scale uniformly from this constant.
*   **Speed Limits:** 400 m/s soft cruise. 1,600 m/s Boost (allowed anywhere outside atmospheres). 2,000 m/s absolute hard cap.
*   **Origin Rebasing:** The 32-bit Rapier physics bubble *never* rotates. Its axes are always parallel to the Sun frame. Origin shifts happen in exact multiples of 256m () to ensure bit-perfect float64-to-float32 handoffs without phantom momentum spikes.
*   **Zero-GC Allocation:** All ArrayBuffers for terrain generation are allocated at boot. Web Workers use `Transferable` objects to pass geometry. Main Thread updates Three.js meshes by swapping `.array` references in place and setting `needsUpdate = true`. `geometry.dispose()` is strictly forbidden mid-flight.


## 4. Out of Scope (Hooks Required)
The following features will not be built in Milestone 1, but the architecture must leave clean hooks for them:
*   Damage, health, crashing, and respawning.
*   Audio and music engines.
*   Fuel (thrust is currently unlimited).
*   Astronaut jetpack (currently only has a limited RCS pack for zero-g recovery).
*   Swimming, climbing, and flying controllers.
*   Asteroid belts, comets, and real-date starting arrangements.


## 5. Glossary
*   **Floating Origin:** Render and physics space re-centered dynamically in 256m increments to prevent 32-bit float jitter.
*   **Macro/Micro Time:** 30x time-dilation for orbits; 60Hz 1x real-time for rigid body physics.
*   **Sky Impostor:** A 2D raymarched cone projected onto a billboard that perfectly matches the pixel boundaries of a distant 3D planet.
*   **Kinematic Astronaut:** The player character is a kinematic controller. The planet's 52 m/s rotational velocity is handled analytically during the state-sync, never injected into Rapier as physical speed.
*   **Pause-and-Prime:** The strictly gated loading sequence that ensures a saved game at 2,000 m/s does not resume physics until Web Workers have generated the collision meshes in the immediate flight path.
